Amazfit Bip 6 Review: 14-Day Battery and Big AMOLED Under $150

Often available around $70–$80, the Amazfit Bip 6 pairs a bright 1.97-inch AMOLED screen with free offline maps, Bluetooth calling, and multi-week battery life that few rivals match at this price.

Day 26 Spotlight • Amazon 4 min read

The Amazfit Bip 6 stands out in the under-$150 category by delivering a genuinely large, sharp AMOLED display without forcing daily charging. The 1.97-inch panel is bright enough for outdoor use and supports a wide selection of customizable faces, making it easy to glance at stats or notifications without unlocking a phone.

Battery life is the headline feature. Amazfit rates it for up to 14 days of typical use, and real-world mixed tracking with occasional GPS still clears a full week for most people. That is a clear advantage over many watches that demand a charger every night or two. The magnetic charging cable is simple and reliable.

Built-in dual-band GPS plus free downloadable offline maps is rare at this price. You can follow turn-by-turn routes on the watch itself for runs, walks, or rides without relying on a phone signal. Bluetooth calling and text replies (with quick responses) further reduce the need to pull out a phone during workouts.

Health tracking covers continuous heart rate, blood oxygen, sleep stages, stress, and more than 140 workout modes including strength training. Accuracy is solid for everyday fitness rather than clinical precision, and the Zepp app organizes the data cleanly on both iOS and Android. Water resistance reaches 5 ATM, suitable for swimming and showering.

At roughly $79 (and frequently less on sale), the Bip 6 offers one of the best balances of screen size, battery endurance, GPS navigation, and calling features available under $150. It lacks the deepest smart-app ecosystem of Wear OS or watchOS, yet for straightforward fitness tracking and long wear time it is hard to beat.
