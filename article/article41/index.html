Samsung Galaxy A16 5G Review: Bright AMOLED 5G Phone Under $150

Often found between $120–$149 on Amazon (including renewed and carrier deals), the Galaxy A16 5G brings a sharp Super AMOLED screen, solid battery life, 5G connectivity, and Samsung’s multi-year update promise to the sub-$150 range.

Day 41 Spotlight • Amazon 4 min read

The 6.7-inch Super AMOLED display is the standout feature. Running at 90Hz with Full HD+ resolution (1080x2340), it delivers vibrant colors, deep blacks, and good outdoor visibility up to around 800 nits. For a phone in this price bracket, the panel feels noticeably more premium than typical LCD screens found on many budget competitors, making videos, browsing, and messaging more enjoyable.

Battery life holds up well for daily use. The 5000mAh cell easily covers a full day of mixed tasks including social media, calls, navigation, and streaming, with many users reporting solid two-day stretches under lighter loads. 25W wired charging is adequate for topping up, and the IP54 splash resistance adds a bit of peace of mind for everyday accidents.

The camera system is practical rather than exceptional. A 50MP main sensor handles daylight shots with decent detail and natural color, while the 5MP ultrawide and 2MP macro cover basic wide-angle and close-up needs. Night performance is limited, as expected at this price, but the results are usable for casual photography and social sharing. Video tops out at 1080p30 with electronic stabilization.

Performance is tuned for everyday tasks. The chipset (Exynos 1330 or Dimensity 6300 depending on region) with 4GB or more of RAM keeps apps responsive, web browsing smooth, and light multitasking comfortable. Heavier games will show their limits, but the phone is reliable for the core smartphone experience most buyers need. Expandable storage via microSD is a welcome inclusion.

Software is a quiet strength. Samsung promises up to six years of OS and security updates, a rare commitment in the under-$150 segment. One UI remains clean and feature-rich, with useful tools like Edge panels, customizable routines, and solid integration with the Galaxy ecosystem. 5G connectivity, NFC, and side-mounted fingerprint reader round out a well-balanced package that prioritizes long-term usability over raw speed.
