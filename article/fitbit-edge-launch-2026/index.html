Fitbit Edge Is Official: $180, 7-Day Battery, and a Screen That Actually Matters

Google launched the Fitbit Edge on October 12. It sits between the screenless Fitbit Air and the full Pixel Watch — exactly the middle ground many people have been asking for.

Published October 13, 2026 • 8:20 PM EDT6 min read
Field note Fitbit Edge Launch

NOTE FACTS

  • Price: $179.99 (Stephen Curry edition $219.99)
  • Battery: up to 7 days (2 days with always-on display)
  • Display: 1.34-inch AMOLED, always-on capable
  • First Fitbit with a user-replaceable battery
  • Pre-order now, ships October 27

The teaser from last week is now a real product. Google officially announced the Fitbit Edge on Monday, October 12, with pre-orders open immediately and retail availability set for October 27. The price is $179.99 (or $219.99 for the Stephen Curry special edition).

This is the first Fitbit tracker with a proper color screen since the Charge 6, and the first Fitbit ever with a user-replaceable battery. Google rates it for up to seven days of normal use, dropping to about two days if you leave the always-on display running. A five-minute charge is claimed to deliver a full day of tracking.

The hardware is deliberately slim: 1.34-inch AMOLED display, 11.5 mm thick, 17.7 g without the band, IP68 and 5 ATM water resistance. Built-in GPS is claimed to be 40 % more accurate than the previous generation. Sensors cover continuous heart rate, SpO2, skin temperature, ECG for atrial fibrillation checks, and altimeter. Automatic workout detection now covers up to 100 activity types with Gemini-powered coaching prompts on the wrist.

On the forums the reaction has been consistent with the broader 2026 conversation. People who already own multi-day Amazfit or Huawei watches are mostly interested in whether the Edge finally gives them a Google-branded option that does not need a nightly charge. Users who liked the Charge series but hated the aging hardware see this as the logical upgrade. The $80 jump from the screenless Fitbit Air is the main debate: if you leave the phone at home for runs or want glanceable stats and notifications, the screen and GPS are worth it. If you mainly care about sleep and recovery, the Air still does the same core job for less.

For the budget-conscious smartwatch shopper the Edge lands just outside the strict $150 ceiling, but it is still the closest Google has come to the “long battery + real screen” formula that Amazfit has been winning with for years. The replaceable battery is the detail that may matter most over a three- or four-year ownership cycle. Pre-orders include three months of Google Health Premium, so early buyers get the coaching features free for the first quarter.

Bottom line: if you want a Fitbit that feels modern, lasts a week, and does not force you into a full smartwatch, the Edge is the one to watch when it arrives on October 27. Until then, the proven Amazfit options under $150 remain the safer daily drivers for most people who already know they hate daily charging.
